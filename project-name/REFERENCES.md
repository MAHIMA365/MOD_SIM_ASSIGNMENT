# References

[1] J. F. Shortle, J. M. Thompson, D. Gross, and C. M. Harris, *Fundamentals of Queueing Theory*, 5th ed. Hoboken, NJ, USA: Wiley, 2018. [Online]. Available: https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119453765

**Project relevance:** This recognised queueing textbook develops Markovian birth-death queues, including M/M/1 and M/M/c models, and explains Erlang C formulas for delay probability and waiting-time measures. It therefore provides the main analytical benchmarks for the one-bay and two-bay comparisons.

[2] S. M. Ross, *Introduction to Probability Models*, 12th ed. Cambridge, MA, USA: Academic Press, 2019. [Online]. Available: https://www.elsevier.com/books/introduction-to-probability-models/ross/978-0-12-814346-9

**Project relevance:** This textbook covers Poisson processes, exponential inter-arrival and service times, continuous-time Markov chains, queueing theory, and simulation. It supports the stochastic assumptions and M/M/1 equations used in the project.

[3] J. Banks, J. S. Carson II, B. L. Nelson, and D. M. Nicol, *Discrete-Event System Simulation*, 5th ed. Upper Saddle River, NJ, USA: Pearson, 2010. [Online]. Available: https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/discrete-event-system-simulation/P200000003161/9780136062127

**Project relevance:** This textbook explains event scheduling, random-variate generation, statistical analysis, verification and validation, and experimental design for discrete-event models. It is directly applicable to advancing the bus-halt model by arrival and departure events.

[4] A. M. Law and W. D. Kelton, *Simulation Modeling and Analysis*, 5th ed. New York, NY, USA: McGraw-Hill Education, 2015. [Online]. Available: https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/simulation-modeling-analysis-law-kelton/M9780073401377.html

**Project relevance:** This is a standard text on constructing and analysing simulation models, including input modelling, output analysis, model verification and validation, and comparison of alternative designs. It supports the experimental and credibility checks required for the M/M/1 versus M/M/2 study.

[5] N. Metropolis and S. Ulam, "The Monte Carlo method," *J. Amer. Stat. Assoc.*, vol. 44, no. 247, pp. 335-341, Sep. 1949, doi: 10.1080/01621459.1949.10483310.

**Project relevance:** This peer-reviewed paper is the foundational publication defining Monte Carlo methods for problems handled by sampling and statistical estimation. It supports interpreting repeated random simulation runs and estimating queue performance measures.

[6] R. G. Sargent, "Verification and validation of simulation models," *J. Simulation*, vol. 7, no. 1, pp. 12-24, Feb. 2013, doi: 10.1057/jos.2012.20.

**Project relevance:** This peer-reviewed article presents definitions and methods for simulation verification and validation, including conceptual-model, operational, and data validity. It supports checks that the implementation behaves correctly and that its assumptions and outputs are credible.

[7] L. Kleinrock, *Queueing Systems, Volume I: Theory*. New York, NY, USA: Wiley, 1975.

**Project relevance:** This classic academic text develops queueing models using stochastic processes and provides the theoretical foundation for M/M/1 and multi-server systems. It supports interpretation of queue stability, utilisation, waiting time, and system occupancy.
